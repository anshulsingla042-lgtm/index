About Advocate Vibhor Goel

Advocate Vibhor Goel is a Haryana-based, Delhi-enrolled advocate whose legal training and internships date back to 2017, followed by professional practice since 2022. His experience primarily centres on civil litigation, commercial disputes and property matters, alongside matrimonial, family-law and other statutory proceedings.

His professional exposure includes approximately two years of work involving Rajasthan RERA and Jaipur Development Authority (JDA) matters, followed by litigation-related work before courts in Faridabad, Gurugram, Delhi, Palwal and Nuh. His experience encompasses civil and commercial recovery, commercial suits, contractual and property disputes, arbitration under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, real estate disputes, divorce and mutual-consent proceedings under the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, domestic violence matters, succession, inheritance, mutation, property registration, cheque dishonour, consumer disputes, motor accident claims and selected criminal proceedings.

He holds a B.B.A. LL.B. (Integrated Five-Year Programme) from Bharati Vidyapeeth New Law College, Pune (Kothrud), and has qualified the All India Bar Examination (AIBE-XVII). He has completed the examinations of his two-year LL.M. programme in Criminology at B.R. Ambedkar University Rajasthan, through Maharishi Dayanand Law College, Jaipur, and is awaiting the formal award of the degree.

His professional approach emphasises careful legal analysis, procedural precision and the considered application of statutory provisions and judicial precedents.

Legal Information

General awareness for the public. This is education, not advice on your particular situation.

Know the basics

Your rights if you are arrested
  • You must be told the grounds of arrest (Article 22(1) of the Constitution).
  • You have the right to consult and be defended by an advocate of your choice.
  • You must be produced before a magistrate within 24 hours of arrest, excluding travel time.
  • If you cannot afford an advocate, you can ask for free legal aid.
Registering an FIR

A complaint about a cognizable offence can be given at any police station. You are entitled to a free copy of the FIR. A “Zero FIR” can be registered at any police station regardless of where the offence happened, and is then transferred to the right one. If the police refuse, the complaint can be sent in writing to the Superintendent of Police or taken to a Magistrate.

Bail: the basic idea

Bail is the release of an accused person from custody on conditions while the case continues. For bailable offences it is a matter of right; for non-bailable offences the court uses its discretion, looking at factors such as the seriousness of the charge, the risk of flight and the risk of interfering with evidence. Bail rules differ by case, so seek advice on your facts.

Sending and replying to a legal notice

A legal notice sets out a person’s claim and gives the other side a chance to respond before a case is filed. For some matters, such as a Section 138 cheque dishonour complaint, notice within the time allowed by law is mandatory. If you receive a notice, read it carefully, note any deadline, keep your documents safe and get a reply drafted promptly.

Consumer complaints

If a product is defective or a service is deficient, first write to the seller or provider. If the issue is not resolved, a complaint can be filed with the District Consumer Commission, including online through the e-Daakhil portal. Keep bills, warranty cards and correspondence.

Right to Information (RTI)

Under the Right to Information Act, 2005, any citizen can request information from a public authority by paying a small fee. The authority must generally reply within 30 days. Unsatisfactory replies can be taken in first appeal and then to the Information Commission.

Free legal aid

Under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, free legal services are available to eligible persons, including women, children, persons with disabilities, members of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, and people under a prescribed income limit. Apply through the District Legal Services Authority or call the NALSA helpline, 15100.

The new criminal laws

From 1 July 2024, the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, and the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023, replaced the Indian Penal Code, the Code of Criminal Procedure and the Indian Evidence Act respectively, for offences and proceedings from that date.
